My October Favorites

Below are some things I'm particularly enjoying this month. I hope you enjoy them too!

Books

Here are some books that I've read and liked recently:

  • Last Call: A True Story of Love, Lust, and Murder in Queer New York

    • Author: Elon Green
    • This is a really well-written, sensitive, and humane portrayal of a semi-forgotten series of murders of gay men in New York in the 1990s. The killer was eventually apprehended, but Green is much more interested in the vicims: their lives, the ways they chose to navigate a homophobic world, and the tragedy of their losses. The murderer was known as the "Last Call Killer" because he found his victims in well-known gay bars, many of which are still around today.

  • Exit Strategy

    • Author: Martha Wells
    • I started reading the Murderbot series over the summer and I'm really loving it, even though I don't read much sci-fi. This is the fourth book in the series, which centers on a deadpan, sarcastic security android with a secret soft spot for the humans he's contracted to protect. Come for the intergalactic fight scenes, stay for the surprisingly sweet found-family dynamics and emotional development.

  • No Country for Old Men

    • Author: Cormac McCarthy
    • I'd never read any Cormac McCarthy before this, so I thought I'd begin with what people refer to as his most "accessible" work. I liked it; he has a distinctive voice and style and the story drives forward at a ruthless, but not breakneck, pace. I'd read more from him.

  • The Marriage Portrait

    • Author: Maggie O'Farrell
    • Another beautifully-written novel from the author of Hamnet. This is the story of Lucrezia de'Medici, a teenage noblewoman in Renaissance Florence who must navigate a dangerous marriage to a powerful duke. It's slow to get going, but once it does, you're in for a ride. The ending is perfect.
